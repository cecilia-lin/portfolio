The secret life of blood glucose

An interactive data story that makes continuous glucose-monitor data approachable through exploration and visual explanation.

The question

How can a reader explore glucose variation around meals without first needing to understand raw continuous-monitor data?

Context & data

The story visualizes continuous glucose-monitor measurements across 15 participants grouped by diabetic status. The source study used a controlled meal schedule, including a standardized breakfast and lunch.

The interface lets readers explore participant traces, meal events, and distributions of glucose excursion, excursion time, and recovery duration.

My contribution

I co-developed and designed the experience with Guoxuan Xu. My contribution connected Python-based data processing with JavaScript visualizations, turning sensor time series into a guided narrative for a non-technical audience.

Approach

  1. Prepare the telemetry. Process continuous glucose measurements into data usable by the browser.
  2. Introduce the measures. Explain excursion, time to peak, and recovery before asking readers to compare distributions.
  3. Enable exploration. Support participant and group selection, a time-range control, and meal-event overlays.
  4. Connect the story. Pair interactive charts with plain-language explanations.

Evaluation & outcome

The outcome

A working interactive story that lets readers compare participants, adjust the time range, and connect meal events with glucose patterns.

The deliverable demonstrates the full path from preparing time-series data to designing an understandable interface. The public demo is the primary artifact; no usability-study outcome or clinical prediction score is claimed here.

Limitations

The 15-participant view is an exploratory sample. Its patterns do not establish causal effects of meal schedules or support general conclusions about all people in a group.

This portfolio presents the project as data visualization and communication work. Participant matching in the demo is an exploration mechanism, not a validated prediction of an individual’s glucose response.
